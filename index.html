About

I'm an environmental health engineer who has helped redefine engineering as a caring profession, arguing that engineers are obligated to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the public and the planet for present and future generations.
I have spent my career making that case:
  • in the microbiology lab with NSF CAREER support;
  • in villages seeking clean water and nutritious food, recognized with the University of Missouri System's 2019 C. Brice Ratchford Memorial Fellowship;
  • at the U.S. Department of State, where I served from 2014 to 2021 and helped create the world's first parametric insurance for fisheries, supported by a $5 million commitment authorized by Secretary of State John Kerry;
  • in the classroom, earning the 2024 Duncan Fraser Global Award for Excellence in Engineering Education from IFEES;
  • in the governance of my profession, where I co-authored the second edition of the Environmental Engineering Body of Knowledge.

A professor of engineering at Missouri S&T and, by courtesy, of nursing at Duquesne University, I am the first Professional Engineer to hold honorary fellowships in both the American Academy of Nursing and the NLN's Academy of Nursing Education.
I am a leading voice for the convergence of caring and STEM professions. My scholarship challenges traditional professional boundaries without blurring them and prepares professionals to care for the public and the planet in a world reshaped by artificial intelligence.
Trust signals:
  • First person to be dual Board Certified in Environmental Engineering (BCEE) and Environmental Science (BCES);
  • First engineer inducted as Diplomate Laureate of the American Academy of Sanitarians (DLAAS);
  • The only engineer inducted into the inaugural Public Health Academy of the National Academies of Practice (FNAP);
  • First American elected Chair of the Chartered Institute of Environmental Health (CIEH), London.
